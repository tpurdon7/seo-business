# Sales objections and response guide

Use these as conversation structures, not scripts to recite. First acknowledge the concern, ask one useful question, then answer honestly.

## “It is too expensive.”

Ask:

> Compared with what you expected, or compared with another way of doing the work?

Response:

> That makes sense. The price reflects the defined research, implementation and review capacity, rather than a promise of rankings. If the monthly scope is too large, the £1,000 Visibility Sprint is the lower-commitment route: it gives you the diagnosis and plan without committing to ongoing delivery.

Do not discount immediately. Reduce scope before reducing price.

## “Can you guarantee first-page rankings?”

Response:

> No responsible provider can control Google’s rankings or guarantee a position. We can commit to the work: identifying the strongest priorities, improving the site, documenting changes and measuring available signals. If a ranking guarantee is essential, we are not the right fit.

## “How many leads will this generate?”

Response:

> We cannot promise a lead number without reliable baseline data, and even then organic results are not guaranteed. We can check whether tracking is sound, focus work on commercially relevant searches and report tracked enquiries where the data supports it.

Ask:

> Are calls, forms and bookings currently tracked well enough to establish a baseline?

## “How quickly will we see results?”

Response:

> Some fixes and page improvements can be delivered quickly. Search movement usually takes longer and depends on the starting point, competition, implementation and search-engine behaviour. The first month is often about baseline, priority fixes and measurement; an ongoing programme should normally be judged over several months.

Avoid giving a universal month count.

## “We already have an SEO agency.”

Ask:

> What is prompting you to look at this now?

If they want a second opinion:

> The Visibility Sprint can provide an independent view, but we should avoid duplicating work or creating conflicting instructions. We would need clarity on responsibilities and access.

If they are satisfied:

> It may be better to continue with them. I am happy to leave the door open if your needs change.

## “Can you just fix everything in the audit?”

Response:

> The sprint diagnoses and prioritises; implementation is deliberately separate. That keeps the first engagement contained and means you can implement internally, use your developer, or move into a monthly package. If you want implementation included from the start, Growth Visibility may fit better.

## “Why is the Sprint £1,000 when the monthly package is £1,100?”

Response:

> They solve different problems. The Sprint is a concentrated diagnosis with a report and 90-day plan, but no ongoing implementation. Growth Visibility is recurring and includes a monthly strategy cycle, implementation capacity, tracking and reporting. The retainer also assumes a continuing relationship rather than a full standalone diagnostic every month.

## “Can we try one month of the retainer?”

Response:

> We can only agree that if the commercial terms allow it. One month is enough to establish access, baseline and complete initial work, but usually not enough to judge organic-search outcomes. The proposal will state the minimum term and notice period clearly.

Internal note: do not answer until minimum term is confirmed.

## “Another agency offers more content/links for less.”

Response:

> That may be the better fit if volume is the priority. Our scope is based on a smaller number of reviewed, commercially relevant work items and honest authority work. We do not sell a guaranteed quantity of links or publish content simply to hit a volume target.

Ask:

> How are they defining quality, approval and the business outcome behind that volume?

## “Do you use AI to produce the work?”

Response:

> We may use tools to support research, structure or drafting, but client work is reviewed for facts, search quality, brand fit and unsupported claims. We do not publish raw AI output, and regulated or specialist claims require client approval.

## “Can you guarantee we appear in ChatGPT or other AI answers?”

Response:

> No. AI answers vary by product, prompt, location and time, and no agency controls citation. We can improve the clarity, consistency and authority signals that make the business easier for people and machines to understand, then run dated spot-checks to observe changes.

## “We do not want to give you access.”

Response:

> We only request the minimum access needed and prefer named user invitations over shared passwords. The Sprint can proceed with public information, but the findings will be less complete. Ongoing implementation usually requires CMS and measurement access, or a reliable internal person to make the changes.

## “Our developer can make the changes.”

Response:

> That can work well. We can provide clear recommendations and acceptance checks, while your developer implements them. We should define who owns deployment, testing and fixes so nothing falls between teams.

## “We need to speak to the owner/board.”

Response:

> Of course. What information will they need to make a decision, and is there a date when it will be discussed?

Agree a follow-up date. Offer a concise proposal or join a decision call; do not create speculative extra work.

## “Send me some information.”

Ask:

> Happy to. Which is most useful: the one-off Sprint, ongoing implementation, or examples of how the process works?

Then send only the relevant material and agree a follow-up date.

## “We need case studies.”

Response:

> That is a fair request. We will only share work or results we have permission to use, and we will not invent proof. At present we can show `[approved sample/process/proof]`. If that is not enough for your buying process, I understand.

Never imply an unapproved client relationship.

## “Can you do a free audit first?”

Response:

> We can share a limited public observation or automated audit where available. The paid Visibility Sprint is the expert diagnostic: it includes business context, competitor review, prioritisation and a usable action plan.

Do not deliver the paid scope during sales.

## “Can you lower the price?”

Response:

> I would rather reduce or phase the scope so the commitment remains realistic. The options are `[smaller package / fewer deliverables / later start]`. Which constraint matters most?

Any discount must be authorised and recorded with a reason and expiry.

## “We are too busy right now.”

Ask:

> Is the issue internal approval time, implementation capacity, or budget timing?

Response:

> If the team cannot provide access or approvals, starting now may create delay without value. We can schedule a follow-up for `[specific month/date]`, or use the Sprint if a contained planning engagement is still useful.

## “SEO has not worked for us before.”

Ask:

> What was done, for how long, and how was success measured?

Response:

> I would not assume the answer is simply “more SEO”. The Sprint is often the safest route because it lets us inspect the previous work, measurement and commercial priorities before recommending ongoing activity.

## “We only care about enquiries, not rankings.”

Response:

> That is the right commercial focus. Rankings are diagnostic signals, not the final outcome. We should prioritise decision-stage pages and confirm enquiry tracking, while being honest that not every enquiry can be attributed perfectly.

## “Can you work on performance only?”

Response:

> Not under the current packages. Organic performance depends on factors outside our control, including the website, approvals, competition, sales handling and search-engine changes. A fixed scope keeps responsibility clear.

## “What happens if it does not work?”

Response:

> We report what was completed, what the data shows and what remains uncertain. If the evidence does not support the current plan, we change priorities rather than defend activity for its own sake. Cancellation and notice terms are stated in the agreement; outcomes themselves are not guaranteed.

## Follow-up rules

- Record the objection in the pipeline using the prospect’s wording.
- Answer the actual concern, not a rehearsed substitute.
- Never create urgency that is not real.
- Never criticise another provider without evidence.
- Never hide minimum terms, exclusions or dependencies.
- If fit is poor, say so and close the opportunity respectfully.
